The Report Factory

How one small script turns three websites, in three countries, into three inboxes, every single morning.

Three Countries, One Job

MotherboardAgent watches motherboard listings on three online marketplaces, one based in the United States, one in Japan, one in Korea. Every day, without anyone touching a keyboard, it turns what it finds into a dashboard and emails it out.

🔎
Scrape

A program visits each marketplace and copies down prices, ratings, and listings, the kind of scraping a person would otherwise do by hand.

📊
Build a dashboard

The scraped data gets turned into one clean web page that ranks what's new and what's trending.

📧
Email the report

That dashboard gets emailed to the people who need to see it, with zero manual steps.

Three Foreign Bureaus

Picture a newspaper that runs three foreign bureaus: one in New York, one in Tokyo, one in Seoul. Every bureau follows the same editorial template and the same deadline, but each one reads its local instruments differently, star ratings in one city, review counts in another, store counts in a third.

In the code, that's three regions running the exact same steps on three different marketplaces.

While You're Still Asleep

Every weekday morning at 9am Taipei time, without anyone touching a keyboard, a handful of people find an email waiting in their inbox: a ranked list of the newest motherboards in their market. The rest of this module traces exactly how that email gets made.

One project file spells out the whole shape of it, the same four-step pipeline written out three times, once per region.

Scrape, structure it into JSON, build a dashboard, email it. Three regions, one pattern.

One Pattern, Run Three Times At Once

Here's the part that makes it fast: all three regions start at the same moment and none of them waits around for the other two to finish first. Press "Next Step" to watch run_all.sh, the script behind all of this, kick the three bureaus off and watch their reports land in the inboxes.

Reading run_all.sh

The whole trick lives in six lines. Each region's function call ends with a trailing &, which turns it into a background process, and $! hands back its PID so the script can check on it later.

CODE 

run_pipeline "Newegg" scraper.py generate_dashboard.py send_email.py &
PID_NEWEGG=$!
run_pipeline "Kakaku" kakaku_scraper.py generate_kakaku_dashboard.py kakaku_send_email.py &
PID_KAKAKU=$!
run_pipeline "Danawa" danawa_scraper.py generate_danawa_dashboard.py danawa_send_email.py &
PID_DANAWA=$!
PLAIN ENGLISH

Kick off the whole Newegg pipeline, scrape then dashboard then email, but the trailing & backgrounds it so the script doesn't wait for it to finish before moving on.

$! is the PID of the job we just backgrounded. Save it as PID_NEWEGG so we can check on it in a moment.

Same idea, for Kakaku: start its pipeline in the background too.

Save Kakaku's PID as well, as PID_KAKAKU.

And again for Danawa: start its pipeline in the background.

Save Danawa's PID as PID_DANAWA.

💡
Key Insight

Starting a task in the background and checking on it later, instead of doing everything one at a time, is called concurrency. It's the entire reason all three regions can finish around the same time instead of one after another.

Why This Matters When You Talk to AI

Once you can see "one pattern, repeated three times," you know exactly what to ask for next. Adding a fourth region means telling an AI assistant to clone the existing pattern, not invent a new one from scratch.

🚩
Red Flag

If you ever see an AI assistant try to make the three regions share state, or block on each other, that's worth questioning. They were designed to be independent, and forcing them to coordinate defeats the entire point of running them at once.
